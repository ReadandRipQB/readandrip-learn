What is Cover 2? It's a zone defense where two safeties split the deep part of the field in half, the Mike runs the deep middle between them, and four defenders cover the short zones underneath. If you can see Cover 2 before the snap, you already know where the two best throws on the field are.

Here's how I draw it on the board, where it's soft, and what it looks like before the ball is snapped.

## What is Cover 2: two deep halves, four under

The number tells you how many defenders play deep. In Cover 2, that's the two safeties:

- **The free safety and the strong safety** each take a deep half of the field, from the hash out to the sideline.

That leaves the rest of the coverage. On my board:

- **Each corner** sits shallow and takes the **flat** on his side.
- **The Sam and the Will** take the **curl** zones.
- **The Mike** turns and runs to the **deep middle**, right between the two safeties.

The four defensive linemen rush the quarterback.

Every one of those seven defenders is covering a spot on the field, not a man. That's what makes it a zone.

## Why defenses play it

Cover 2 takes away the easy throws. The corners sit right on top of the flats, so the quick out and the bubble are covered before they start. With the Sam and Will in the curls, the short middle is crowded too.

The trade is up top. Two safeties have to cover the whole deep field, and each one has a lot of grass to protect.

## Where the holes are

Cover 2 has two holes:

- **The middle of the field.** The two safeties split the field in half, so the middle is the space between them. The Mike runs there to cover it, and he has a long way to go.
- **The honey hole.** The space between the safety and the corner, deep along the sideline. The corner is sitting low in the flat, and the safety has a long way to run to get there. That's the spot on my board with the circle around it.

## What to look for before the snap

1. **Count the safeties.** Two safeties deep and level with each other is your first sign. Two high usually means Cover 2, Cover 4 or Cover 6.
2. **Look at the corners.** In Cover 2 the corners are usually close to the line, outside the receiver, with their eyes on the quarterback. They want to push the receiver inside and sit in the flat.
3. **Look at the safety depth.** Cover 2 safeties are usually a little deeper and wider than Quarters safeties, because each one owns half the field.
4. **Use motion.** Send a receiver across. If nobody runs with him and the defense just shifts, think zone.

Defenses disguise this. A defense can show two high and rotate to one high at the snap. Your pre-snap read tells you where to look. The snap tells you if you were right.

## Cover 2 vs Cover 3

Before the snap, Cover 2 usually shows two high safeties and Cover 3 usually shows one. After the snap, count the deep defenders: two splitting the field in half is Cover 2, three splitting it in thirds is Cover 3. ## FAQ

### Is Cover 2 man or zone?

Zone. The two safeties, two corners and three linebackers each cover an area of the field, not a receiver.

### What is the honey hole in Cover 2?

It's the space deep along the sideline, between where the corner sits in the flat and where the safety is covering from the middle of his half. It's the most common place to attack Cover 2.

### How do I tell Cover 2 from Cover 4?

Both show two high safeties. In Cover 2 the corners are usually close to the line, ready to sit in the flats. In Cover 4 the corners usually line up deeper, because each one is playing a deep quarter.
