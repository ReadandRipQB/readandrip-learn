What is Cover 1? It's man coverage with one free safety deep in the middle of the field: every receiver has a defender on him, and that one safety helps over the top. If Cover 3 is the zone you'll see most, Cover 1 is the man coverage you'll see most, so a young quarterback has to know both cold.

Here's how I draw it on the board, why defenses call it, where it breaks, and how to tell it's coming before the ball is snapped.

## What is Cover 1: man on every receiver, one safety deep

The number tells you how many defenders play deep. In Cover 1, that's one:

- **The free safety** stays deep in the middle of the field. He doesn't have a man. His job is to help on anything thrown deep.
- **Everyone else in coverage has a man.** The corners take the outside receivers, the nickel takes the slot, the strong safety takes the tight end, and a linebacker takes the running back.

That leaves one defender with no receiver. Depending on the call, he either rushes the quarterback or sits in the short middle of the field to rob crossing routes. Coaches often call that player the "robber."

## Why defenses call Cover 1

Cover 1 lets a defense get tight on receivers and still keep one player deep as insurance. It also frees an extra defender to rush or to sit in the middle, which helps against the run and against short crossing routes.

The trade is simple: every defender is on an island. If a receiver beats his man, there may be nobody close enough to help except one safety who has the whole deep field.

## Where Cover 1 is weak

- **Rub and pick concepts.** Receivers cross close to each other so a man defender has to fight through traffic to stay with his guy. One step behind is all a receiver needs.
- **Mesh.** Two receivers cross each other underneath, going opposite ways. Every man defender has to chase his receiver across the field and through the crowd in the middle.
- **The running back on a linebacker.** In Cover 1 a linebacker usually has the back. Get the running back out into a route and you've got a back matched up on a linebacker in space.

## What to look for before the snap

1. **Count the safeties.** One safety deep in the middle (one high) means Cover 1 or Cover 3. That alone doesn't tell you which.
2. **Use motion.** Send a receiver across the formation. If a defender runs across with him, think man, which with one high safety points to Cover 1. If nobody follows and the defense just shifts, think zone (Cover 3).
3. **Watch the defenders' eyes and alignment.** Man defenders usually line up tight on their receiver and stare at him. Zone defenders line up deeper and look at the quarterback.
4. **Count the box.** If the second safety has walked down near the line, one more defender is up close to rush or rob. Know who is free.

Defenses disguise it, so your pre-snap read tells you where to look, and the snap tells you whether you were right.

## Cover 1 vs Cover 3

Both look like one high safety before the snap. The difference is what everyone else does after it. In Cover 1, defenders follow receivers. In [Cover 3]({{ '/what-is-cover-3/' | relative_url }}), they drop to spots on the field. Motion is the fastest way to tell them apart.

## FAQ

### Is Cover 1 man or zone?

Man. Every defender in coverage follows a receiver, except the free safety, who plays the deep middle.

### What is the difference between Cover 0 and Cover 1?

Cover 1 keeps one free safety deep to help over the top. Cover 0 has no deep help at all; every defender is in man or rushing, so the ball has to come out fast.

### Who is the "robber" in Cover 1?

It's the one defender left over after everyone has a man. Depending on the call, he rushes the quarterback or sits in the short middle of the field to take away crossing routes.
