Can you name the coverage?

Ten whiteboards. Each one shows a defense. Name what you're looking at, then see why. Built for players, parents and coaches. No sign-up.

The coverages in this quiz

Every coverage answers one question first: how many defenders are playing deep, and what are the rest doing? Count the deep players and check whether the underneath defenders are watching a receiver or a spot on the field.

Cover 0

No deep safety. Everyone in coverage is in man, and the defense sends extra rushers. The ball has to come out fast.

Cover 1

One deep safety in the middle of the field and man coverage underneath.

Cover 2

Two deep safeties, each with half the field. Five defenders underneath play zones.

Cover 3

Three deep defenders, each with a third of the field, usually the two corners and a safety. Four defenders underneath play zones.

Cover 4 (Quarters)

Four deep defenders, each with a quarter of the field: both corners and both safeties. Three defenders underneath.

Cover 6

Quarters to one side and Cover 2 to the other. Two deep quarter players on one half, one deep half player on the other, with the corner on the half side sitting in the flat.

Single-high and two-high

Before the snap, look at the safeties. One safety deep in the middle is a single-high shell, which usually means Cover 1 or Cover 3. Two safeties deep is a two-high shell, which usually means Cover 2, Cover 4 or Cover 6. Defenses can rotate after the snap, so the shell is where the read starts, not where it ends.

How to read the whiteboards

The offense is at the bottom. Guards and tackles are circles, the center is the box with an X, and everyone else is a letter. Defenders are letters too: C for corners, F and S for safeties, W, M and N for linebackers and the nickel, E and T for the defensive line. Shaded boxes and ovals are zones. A dashed line means a defender is in man coverage on that player. Arrows are rushers.
