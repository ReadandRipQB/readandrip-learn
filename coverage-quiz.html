Can you name the coverage?

Ten whiteboards. Each one shows a defense. Name what you're looking at, then see why. Built for players, parents and coaches. No sign-up.

The coverages in this quiz

Every coverage answers one question first: how many defenders are playing deep, and what are the rest doing? Count the deep players, then check whether the defenders underneath are running to a spot on the field or following a receiver.

Cover 0

Nobody deep. The corners and both safeties are in man on the receivers, with the safeties playing off over the #2 receivers. The Will, Mike and Sam are in the box, and the defense sends extra rushers. The ball has to come out fast.

Cover 1

One high safety in the middle of the field. Everyone else in coverage is in man on a receiver.

Cover 2

Both safeties get a deep half. The corners take the flats, two defenders take the curls, and the Mike runs the deep middle.

Cover 3

The free safety and both corners each get a deep third. Underneath, two defenders play curl-flat and two play hook-curl.

Cover 4 (Quarters)

Both corners and both safeties each get a deep quarter of the field, with three defenders underneath.

Cover 6

Quarters to one side and Cover 2 to the other: a corner and safety split the deep half on one side in quarters, the other safety takes the deep half on the other side, and the corner on that side takes the flat.

Single-high and two-high

Before the snap, look at the safeties. One safety deep in the middle is a single-high shell, which usually means Cover 1 or Cover 3. Two safeties deep is a two-high shell, which usually means Cover 2, Cover 4 or Cover 6. Defenses can rotate after the snap, so the shell is where the read starts, not where it ends.

How to read the whiteboards

The offense is at the bottom. Guards and tackles are circles, the center is the box with an X, and everyone else is a letter. On defense, C is a corner, FS and SS are the free and strong safety, W, M and S are the Will, Mike and Sam linebackers, and E and T are the defensive line. Arrows show where each defender goes after the snap. A dotted line means a defender is in man coverage on that receiver.
